Youngest Planet Ever Confirmed by Astronomers

📅 Trending on September 27, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read 🏷 Science / Space

Astronomers have confirmed the youngest planet ever found. The planet, named Elias 2-24 b, is less than one million years old. It is still growing inside a disk of gas and dust around its young star.

What Makes This Planet Special

Most planets we know are billions of years old. Even the previous youngest ones were about five million years old. Elias 2-24 b is much younger. It sits about 450 light-years from Earth in a star-forming region called Ophiuchus.

The planet is roughly the size of Jupiter. It is still pulling in material from the surrounding disk. Scientists say this gives them a rare look at a planet in the middle of being born.

How It Was Found

A team led by doctoral student Andrea Bernardi studied old telescope data. They used images from the W. M. Keck Observatory in Hawaii. Other data came from the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) and the Very Large Telescope in Chile.

The planet sits in a clear gap in the dusty disk around its star. Planets often carve out these gaps as they form. The team tracked the faint point of light over several years. Its motion matched that of a planet orbiting the star, not a background object.

Why It Matters

Current models of how planets form already had trouble explaining the previous young planets. This new one is even younger and sits far from its star. It shows that giant planets can grow faster than scientists expected.

The discovery helps explain the gaps seen in many young disks. It also supports the idea that large planets form by building a solid core first and then gathering gas around it.

Looking Ahead

Elias 2-24 b is still gathering mass. Future observations will show how it continues to change. Researchers say this is just the start of finding more baby planets.

The result was published in The Astrophysical Journal Letters. It gives scientists a fresh view of the earliest stages of planet formation.
