The “Bassline” Skincare Hijack — How Vaseline Turned a Viral Mispronunciation Into a Campaign

📅 Trending on August 27, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read 🏷 Viral / Internet Culture

In late August 2026, a simple skincare video turned into one of the internet’s funniest brand moments.

Korean creator Min Day Lee shared a clip showing off her big collection of Vaseline products. While talking about them, she pronounced the brand name as “Baseline.” Viewers heard it clearly. The sound stuck. Soon people started calling it “Bassline.”

The video exploded. Millions of views poured in. Creators remixed the audio. Memes spread fast. People put the funny pronunciation into all kinds of videos. What started as a normal product showcase became a full internet trend.

How the Brand Reacted

Instead of ignoring it or correcting people, Vaseline joined the fun. The brand posted playful messages using the new name. They joked about an official rebrand to “Baseline.” They even called Min Day Lee and other creators “Vaseline-Verified Hijack Icons.”

The company invited fans to make their own versions with the hashtag #VaselineHijacked. Official posts leaned fully into the joke with captions full of energy and humor.

Why It Worked

The moment felt real and light. No big campaign planned it. A creator’s natural way of saying the name connected with people. Vaseline showed it could laugh along and join the conversation. That smart move turned a random viral clip into positive attention for the brand.

By August 27, “Bassline” was everywhere on social media. A simple pronunciation had hijacked the skincare giant’s feed — and the brand happily let it happen.
