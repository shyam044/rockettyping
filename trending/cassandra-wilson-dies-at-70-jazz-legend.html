Cassandra Wilson Death: Jazz Legend Passes at 70

📅 Trending on September 3, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read 🏷 News / Obituaries

Cassandra Wilson, the celebrated jazz singer known for her deep and unique voice, has died. She was 70 years old.

Wilson passed away in her hometown of Jackson, Mississippi. Her longtime manager shared that she died peacefully at home, surrounded by family, close friends, and her manager. No cause of death has been made public.

A Distinguished Career

Born in Jackson in 1955, Wilson grew up surrounded by music. Her father was a jazz musician, and she began playing piano and guitar at a young age. She later moved to New York and became an important part of the jazz scene.

She was known for blending traditional jazz with blues, folk, country, and other styles. Her low, smoky voice stood out and earned her wide praise. Time magazine once called her America’s best singer.

Wilson won two Grammy Awards. One came for her 1995 album New Moon Daughter. Another followed later for Loverly. She recorded many well-loved albums and performed with major artists throughout her career.

She also received recognition as an NEA Jazz Master, one of the highest honors in jazz.

Tributes and Legacy

Jackson Mayor John Horhn described her as an extraordinary native daughter whose music carried the sound and spirit of Mississippi. Musicians and fans around the world have shared memories of her artistry and influence.

Wilson’s work helped expand what jazz singing could sound like. She leaves behind a rich catalog of recordings that continue to inspire listeners.

Her family has asked for privacy during this time. Further details about memorial services will be shared later.
