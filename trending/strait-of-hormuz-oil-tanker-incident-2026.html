Iran's IRGC Claims Strike on Togo-Flagged Oil Tanker in Strait of Hormuz

📅 Trending on September 18, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read 🏷 News / World

On September 18, 2026, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said its navy struck a Togo-flagged oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz.

The tanker, named Trend, was hit while trying to pass through the busy waterway. According to the IRGC, a fire broke out on the ship and it was forced to stop.

What the IRGC Said

The IRGC Navy stated that the tanker attempted an "illegal" transit. It claimed the vessel was guided or provoked by the US military.

Iranian officials said their forces still control the Strait of Hormuz and will not allow unauthorized ships to pass. They warned that any illegal attempt would lead to the destruction of the vessel.

Other Reports

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported a security incident in the same area. It said the event happened about 16 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman.

UKMTO noted that the crew was safe and there was no confirmed environmental damage at the time of the report. Authorities are looking into the details.

Why This Matters

The Strait of Hormuz is a key route for oil shipments around the world. Many tankers pass through it every day. Attacks like this raise concerns about shipping safety and can affect oil prices.

This incident is part of ongoing tensions in the region linked to the wider conflict. Shipping companies have been advised to stay careful and report any unusual activity.

No independent confirmation of the full details of the strike has been widely released yet. Updates may come as more information is checked by maritime groups and officials.

Ships in the area continue to face higher risks, and global markets are watching closely for any impact on oil supplies.
