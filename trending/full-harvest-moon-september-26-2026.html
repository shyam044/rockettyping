The Full Harvest Moon Rises on September 26

📅 Trending on September 26, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read 🏷 Science / Space

This weekend brings a special full moon. The Full Harvest Moon reaches its peak on Saturday, September 26, 2026. It is the full moon closest to the autumn equinox and marks the first full moon of fall in the Northern Hemisphere.

What Is the Harvest Moon?

The Harvest Moon is not a different kind of moon. It is simply the name given to the full moon that falls nearest to the start of autumn. In 2026, that date is September 26.

Long ago, farmers used the bright light of this moon to work later in the fields during harvest time. The extra evening light helped them gather crops after sunset. That is how the name began.

When and How to See It

The moon becomes fully illuminated at 12:49 p.m. Eastern Time (16:49 UTC) on September 26. At that moment it is still below the horizon for most people in North America and Europe.

The best time to watch is shortly after sunset on the evening of September 26. Look toward the eastern horizon. The moon will rise low in the sky and often look large and golden or orange. This color comes from the way Earth’s atmosphere scatters light when the moon is near the horizon.

On the following evenings, the moon rises only a short time later than the night before. This is the classic Harvest Moon effect. It gives several nights of early evening moonlight.

Extra Sky Highlights

Saturn will shine near the moon this year. The bright planet appears just below or beside the full moon in the evening sky. Fainter Neptune is also nearby, though you will need binoculars or a telescope to spot it.

Tips for Viewing

  • Find a spot with a clear view of the eastern sky and little light pollution.
  • No special equipment is needed. Your eyes are enough.
  • Arrive a few minutes before moonrise for the best color and size effect.
  • Check local moonrise and sunset times for your area.

The Full Harvest Moon is a simple and beautiful event. Step outside on the evening of September 26, look east, and enjoy the bright autumn moon lighting up the sky.
