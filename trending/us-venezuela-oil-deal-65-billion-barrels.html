U.S.–Venezuela Oil Deal Explained: Trump’s 65 Billion Barrel Agreement

📅 Trending on August 29, 2026 ⏱ 2 min read 🏷 News / World

On August 28 and 29, 2026, news broke of a major energy agreement between the United States and Venezuela.

U.S. President Donald Trump called it the biggest oil deal in world history. He said the deal gives the U.S. majority control over more than 65 billion barrels of Venezuela’s proven oil reserves. The arrangement involves a partnership with private companies and is said to cost American taxpayers nothing.

Venezuela’s interim President Delcy Rodriguez welcomed the deal. She described it as historic. According to her, the agreement covers the development of 17 key oil fields. The goal is to raise production toward 1.5 million barrels per day. She said the deal will last 25 years and keep Venezuela in control of its natural resources.

What Both Sides Expect

For the U.S., leaders say the deal can bring more oil supply, help lower gas prices over time, and strengthen energy security. For Venezuela, officials hope it will bring large investment (possibly over $100 billion), create jobs, boost government revenue, and help revive the oil industry after years of problems.

Details are still limited. Full documents have not been widely released. Experts note that turning the agreement into real production will take time and face practical challenges like infrastructure needs.

Why It Matters

Venezuela holds some of the world’s largest oil reserves, but output has stayed low for years. This deal marks a big shift in relations between the two countries. It has sparked wide discussion about energy, investment, and future oil markets.

The story continues to develop as more information becomes available.
