SpaceX Starship Reaches Earth Orbit for the First Time

📅 Trending on September 28, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read 🏷 Science / Space

SpaceX’s Starship rocket reached Earth orbit on Monday, September 28, 2026. This was the vehicle’s 14th test flight and its first successful trip into orbit.

The Launch

The rocket lifted off from SpaceX’s Starbase site in South Texas. The Super Heavy booster powered the vehicle upward with its many engines. After separation, the Starship upper stage continued into space.

One engine shut down early during the climb. At first, it looked like the mission might not reach orbit. Engineers checked the data and decided the vehicle was still healthy enough to try. A short engine burn then pushed Starship into a stable orbit around Earth.

What Happened in Orbit

Once in orbit, Starship deployed 26 new Starlink satellites. These are the advanced Version 3 models. They will join SpaceX’s growing internet network in space. This was the first time Starship carried operational satellites all the way to orbit.

The plan called for several loops around Earth before a controlled return. The booster had already splashed down safely in the Gulf of Mexico.

Why This Matters

Previous Starship flights stayed on shorter paths and never fully circled the planet. Reaching orbit is a major step. It shows the rocket can do the basic job needed for future missions.

Starship is designed to carry heavy loads, including more Starlink satellites, and later support trips to the Moon and Mars. Success on this flight brings those goals closer.

Looking Ahead

SpaceX will study the flight data closely. The company will use what it learned to improve the next vehicles. More test flights are already planned.

For now, Starship has proven it can reach orbit. That simple achievement marks a clear milestone after years of testing.
