🖐️ Lesson: Type ASDF

ASDF uses the four main left-hand home-row fingers: A with your left pinky, S with your left ring finger, D with your left middle finger, and F with your left index finger. These exercises build your left-hand coordination and muscle memory from simple movements to longer ASDF patterns. Keep your eyes on the text above, not on your hands.

How to Type ASDF Correctly

The letters A, S, D, and F are the four main left-hand home-row anchor keys used in touch typing. Press A with your left pinky, S with your left ring finger, D with your left middle finger, and F with your left index finger.

Keep your left hand relaxed on the home row and allow each finger to return to its natural position after pressing a key. The raised mark on the F key can help you locate the home-row position without looking down.

This lesson uses five progressive drills. You begin by moving across the four home-row keys, then practice two-letter combinations, three-letter patterns, complete four-letter sequences, and finally longer repeated patterns. Focus on accuracy and consistent finger movement before trying to increase your speed.

Which fingers type ASDF?

Your left pinky types A, your left ring finger types S, your left middle finger types D, and your left index finger types F. These four fingers should remain relaxed and return to their home-row positions after each keystroke.

Why practice ASDF together?

ASDF is the foundation of the left-hand home row. Practicing these four keys together develops finger independence, coordination, accuracy, and muscle memory before you move on to more advanced typing patterns.
